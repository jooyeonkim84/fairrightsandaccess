You’re right to push on “numbers don’t lie” — the problem is Google AI mixed up what the numbers mean (and it flat-out invented/misread the GPU clock).

 

Here’s the truth, with the “unit decoding”:

1) The 4266 number for Helio G99 is real — but it’s Mbps/MT/s, not “4266 MHz”

 

MediaTek’s own Helio G99 page says Max Memory Frequency: 4266 Mbps (LPDDR4X).

 

LPDDR works like DDR: the effective data rate is about 2× the clock.

So “LPDDR4X-4266” corresponds to about 2133 MHz clock — which is exactly why spec sites also list 2133 MHz memory frequency for Helio G99.

 

✅ So Google AI saying “4266 MHz RAM” is the wrong unit, even though the 4266 figure exists.

2) The Pro’s “3200 MHz” is also almost certainly a unit mismatch

 

LPDDR5 commonly gets reported as 3200 MHz clock, which corresponds to ~6400 MT/s effective (again, DDR-style). Notebookcheck references LPDDR5-6400 for Dimensity 7050-class parts.

 

So “S118 Pro 3200” does not automatically mean it’s slower than “S118 4266” — it’s very likely:

 

    S118: 4266 MT/s effective (≈2133 MHz clock)

 

    Pro: 3200 MHz clock (≈6400 MT/s effective)

 

That would mean the Pro’s memory system is not inherently slower based on those numbers.

3) The “Mali-G57 turbo 2133 MHz” claim is basically bogus

 

Helio G99’s GPU is Mali-G57 MP2, and typical listed GPU clocks are around ~1000 MHz (not 2133).

Dimensity 7050’s Mali-G68 MC4 is commonly listed around ~950 MHz.

 

So Google AI’s “2133 MHz GPU turbo” is almost certainly confusing the RAM clock (2133) with the GPU clock.

4) What this means for your conclusion

 

    Your “S118 = UX-oriented because faster RAM/GPU clocks” argument is not supported by accurate interpretation of the specs.

 

    It’s still totally possible your S118 feels smoother in real life — but the likely reasons are firmware tuning, thermals/throttling behavior, storage speed, background apps, and having 12GB physical RAM (fewer reloads), not “2133 MHz GPU turbo.”

 

If you want to settle it with actual numbers (not marketing/spec-table confusion): run Geekbench 6 + 3DMark Wild Life + a storage test on your S118, and compare to S118 Pro results (or run them after you upgrade). That will tell you what’s real on your unit.
